# Glossary — SynapseOps

This document defines the authoritative terminology used throughout the SynapseOps project. Consistent use of these terms across documentation, code, and discussion is important for clarity and precision. When a term is ambiguous in context, its definition here takes precedence.

---

## A

### Action Executor
The component responsible for carrying out authorized recovery actions against the target infrastructure. Receives structured, validated action representations from the authorization layer. Does not accept free-form instructions from AI components.

### AIOps
Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations. A broad discipline covering the application of ML, AI, and data analytics to IT operations processes including event correlation, anomaly detection, root cause analysis, and automated remediation. SynapseOps is an experimental AIOps system.

### Anomaly
A deviation from the expected or historically normal behavior of a system metric, log pattern, or event sequence. An anomaly is a signal — it may indicate an incident, a change in workload, or expected system evolution. Anomalies are not the same as incidents or root causes.

### Anomaly Detection
The process of identifying signals that deviate significantly from modeled normal behavior. SynapseOps aims to use statistical and ML-based approaches for anomaly detection rather than purely threshold-based alerting.

### Authorization
The process of granting or denying a proposed action based on policy rules, action risk level, system state, and optionally explicit human approval. Authorization is architecturally separate from AI reasoning.

### Autonomy
The capacity of the system to take operational actions without human intervention in a specific instance. SynapseOps autonomy is bounded by policy, risk level, and the action authorization model. Unrestricted autonomy is explicitly prohibited.

---

## C

### Confidence
A measure of the system's estimated certainty in a hypothesis, classification, or proposed action. Confidence should be calibrated against historical accuracy. High-confidence actions may be authorized differently than low-confidence actions.

### Correlation
The process of associating multiple events, alerts, or signals that appear to be related — sharing a common cause, a temporal relationship, or a topological relationship in the dependency graph.

---

## D

### Dependency
A directional relationship between infrastructure components where the correct functioning of one component depends on the availability and health of another. Dependencies define how failures propagate across the system.

### Dependency Graph
A graph-based model of infrastructure components and their dependency relationships. Used by the root cause analysis engine to trace failure propagation and identify probable origin points.

### Detection
The process of identifying that an anomaly or incident is occurring. Detection is separate from diagnosis. A detection says "something is wrong here." Diagnosis attempts to explain why.

---

## E

### Event
A discrete, timestamped occurrence in the infrastructure: a deployment, a health-check state change, an autoscaling action, a container restart, a configuration change. Events are distinct from continuous telemetry signals such as metrics.

### Evidence
The collection of telemetry, events, and state information used to support or refute a hypothesis. SynapseOps should require sufficient evidence before proposing or executing recovery actions.

---

## F

### Feedback
Information about the outcome of an action or decision that can be used to improve future decisions. Feedback may come from automated verification, operator corrections, or explicit ratings.

---

## H

### Human Approval
An explicit authorization signal from a human operator allowing a proposed high-risk action to proceed. Human approval is required for actions above a defined risk threshold. The system should provide the operator with sufficient context (evidence, hypothesis, proposed action, risk assessment) to make an informed decision.

### Hypothesis
A proposed explanation for an observed anomaly or incident, generated by the root cause analysis or AI reasoning layer. A hypothesis should identify a probable root cause, the evidence supporting it, and a confidence estimate.

---

## I

### Incident
A significant degradation or failure in system behavior that requires investigation and potentially corrective action. An incident typically involves one or more anomalies and may span multiple services.

### Infrastructure
The collection of compute, networking, storage, and software components that constitute the operating environment of a software system. In SynapseOps' context, this includes services, databases, caches, load balancers, and their interconnections.

---

## L

### Learning
The process by which the system improves future decisions based on historical outcomes, operator feedback, and accumulated experience. Learning is a long-term capability targeted in Phase 11.

### Log
A textual or structured record of events and state changes generated by a running software component. Logs provide a narrative of system behavior and are a primary telemetry source.

---

## M

### Metric
A numerical measurement of a system property over time: CPU utilization, memory usage, request latency, error rate, queue depth, etc. Metrics are the primary quantitative telemetry source for anomaly detection.

---

## O

### Observability
The property of a system that allows its internal state to be inferred from its external outputs (metrics, logs, traces, events). SynapseOps depends on the target infrastructure being instrumented with sufficient observability tooling.

---

## P

### Policy
A set of rules that govern what actions the system is authorized to take, under what conditions, and subject to what constraints. The policy engine evaluates proposed actions against defined policies before any execution occurs.

---

## R

### Recovery Action
A concrete operational step intended to restore the infrastructure to a healthy state: restarting a service, scaling a deployment, draining a queue, adjusting a configuration value. Recovery actions must be authorized before execution.

### Recovery Plan
A structured proposal consisting of one or more recovery actions, ordered by priority, with associated risk assessments and rationale. Generated by the Recovery Planner and evaluated by the Policy Engine.

### Risk
The potential for negative consequences from executing a proposed action. Risk factors include reversibility, blast radius, confidence in the diagnosis, system criticality, and historical action success rates.

### Root Cause
The fundamental, originating fault that caused an observed incident. Distinguishing root cause from symptoms is a core analytical challenge. A root cause is not always the component that first showed signs of failure.

---

## S

### Self-Healing
The capability of a system to automatically detect failures and take corrective actions to restore desired state without human intervention. SynapseOps aims to explore self-healing within explicit safety constraints and authorization boundaries.

### System State
A structured representation of the current condition of the infrastructure: which services are healthy or degraded, current metric values relative to baselines, known dependency status, and recent event history.

---

## T

### Telemetry
The collective term for all observable signals generated by infrastructure: metrics, logs, traces, and events. Telemetry is the primary input to the SynapseOps observability layer.

### Trace
A record of a request's journey across multiple services in a distributed system, showing latency at each hop, errors encountered, and the full call chain. Traces are particularly useful for diagnosing latency anomalies and cascading failures.

---

## V

### Verification
The process of confirming, through re-observation of the environment, that a recovery action has achieved the desired system state. Verification is distinct from action execution success. A successfully executed action that does not restore system health should trigger escalation.

---

## Notes

- These definitions are living and may be refined as implementation proceeds.
- When code uses terms from this glossary, variable names, class names, and module names should reflect these definitions for consistency.
- New terms introduced during implementation should be added here.
